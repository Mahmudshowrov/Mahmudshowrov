# 3. LinkedIn SEO: Keywords and Placement

## How LinkedIn search actually ranks you

Recruiters search with LinkedIn Recruiter, filtering by **job title, skills, location, years of experience and keywords**. What matters, roughly in order:

1. **Headline and current job title.** These carry the most weight. Put the exact phrases here.
2. **Skills list.** Skills are matched directly to the Skills filter in recruiter searches.
3. **Experience descriptions and the About section.** Full-text keyword match.
4. **Profile completeness and activity.** Active, complete profiles rank higher and show up in "Recruiters may be interested" lists.
5. **Connection proximity.** Recruiters see 2nd-degree connections more easily, so connecting with sourcing recruiters helps directly.

**Rule:** use the **exact phrase** a recruiter would type ("Apparel Sourcing", not just "Sourcing"). Use each main term 2–4 times across the profile. More than that looks like keyword stuffing.

- Merchandising Manager

- Product Development Manager
- Product Manager (Apparel), the title used by many EU brand offices in Dhaka
- Production Manager (buying house side)
- Country Manager / Sourcing Office Manager (5-year target)

## Small details that matter

- **Job title field:** if your contract title is "Senior Merchandiser", keep it exact. Don't inflate it. You can add context after a separator, though, e.g. "Senior Merchandiser — Knit, Woven & Denim". LinkedIn allows this and it adds keywords.
- **Location:** "Dhaka, Bangladesh". Many recruiters filter by location first.
- **Industry:** set it to "Apparel & Fashion" or "Textile Manufacturing".
- **Profile language:** English, with no Bangla in the main fields. (Bangla posts are fine occasionally, but keep most posts in English for an international audience.)
- **Alt text on Featured images and post images:** add keyword-rich descriptions. They help accessibility and give LinkedIn more context about the image.
