# 1. Profile Audit

## 1.1 Overall score: not given yet, on purpose

I can't score a profile I haven't seen, and a made-up number would be worthless to you. Here is the exact rubric I'll use once you send the PDF or screenshots. You can also score yourself against it today; it takes about 10 minutes.

| Area | Weight | Full marks look like this |
|------|-------:|---------------------------|
| Photo | 5 | Head and shoulders, face filling ~60% of the frame, plain background, good light, recent |
| Banner | 5 | Custom, states what you do and for whom, readable on mobile (left side isn't hidden behind the photo) |
| Headline | 15 | Role + categories + markets + value; holds the keywords recruiters search for; not just "Senior Merchandiser at X" |
| About | 15 | First 3 lines hook the reader; specific categories, buyers and markets; at least 2 real numbers; clear call to action with contact details |
| Experience | 20 | Every role has 4–6 achievement bullets, numbers wherever possible, buyer names and product types; no copied job descriptions |
| Skills | 10 | 30–50 relevant skills; top 5 pinned are the ones you want to be hired for; endorsements on the main ones |
| Recommendations | 8 | At least 3: one from a buyer/brand side, one from a manager, one from a factory or a junior |
| Featured | 5 | 3–5 items that prove expertise (posts, a costing breakdown sample, a capability one-pager) |
| Activity | 10 | Posts at least weekly on your own topic area, plus thoughtful comments on industry posts |
| Basics | 7 | Custom URL, location set, industry set, contact info filled in, "Open to" set correctly, education complete |
| **Total** | **100** | |

**What usually happens with Bangladesh merchandiser profiles** (a pattern, not a finding about you): they score 35–55. The usual losses are a job-title-only headline, an empty or generic About, experience copied from a job description, no numbers, no recommendations, and no posts. If yours looks like that, the fixes in [02-profile-rewrite.md](02-profile-rewrite.md) should take you to 75–85 within a week. Getting above 85 takes consistent posting and recommendations, and that takes time.

## 1.2 Section-by-section audit checklist

For each section: what good looks like, the common weak points, and what to add. Once I see your profile I'll mark each item as present, weak or missing.

### Profile photo
- **Good:** professional, warm expression, face fills most of the circle, neutral background. A shirt or blazer is enough; a suit isn't needed.
- **Common weak points:** a factory-floor selfie, a group photo crop, sunglasses, a wedding photo, low resolution, a photo that's 6+ years old.
- **Add:** a background that's plain or lightly blurred. A phone in portrait mode near a window works fine.

### Banner (1584 × 396 px)
- **Common weak points:** LinkedIn's default blue, a random fabric stock image, a company logo only.
- **Add:** see the banner brief in [02-profile-rewrite.md](02-profile-rewrite.md#f-banner). On mobile the photo covers the bottom-left of the banner, so keep text on the right half.

### Headline (220 characters)
- **Common weak point:** "Senior Merchandiser at Nordic Sourcing." This wastes the most-searched field on your profile, because recruiters search by skill and category, not by your employer's name.
- **Add:** categories (Knit / Woven / Denim / Sweater), the function (costing, sourcing, product development), markets (EU/Nordic, US), and the years of experience.

### About (2,600 characters)
- **Common weak points:** opens with "I am a hardworking and dedicated professional…". Only about the first 3 lines show before "see more", and that opening wastes them.
- **Add:** a hook, specifics (categories, buyers, markets), proof (numbers), what you're looking for, and contact details.

### Experience
- **Common weak points:** responsibilities without results ("Responsible for follow-up of orders"); missing buyer names; empty descriptions on older roles.
- **Add:** 4–6 bullets per role, each with an action verb, the task and the result. Add **Skills** to each role (LinkedIn lets you tag up to 5 per role), because that feeds search ranking.
- **Check:** is the Nordic Sourcing entry linked to the company page (logo showing) rather than typed as plain text? Same for Ha-Meem.

### Education
- **Strong point to use:** a CSE degree is rare among merchandisers. Present it as the reason you build costing tools and data dashboards, not as a mismatch with your career.
- **Add:** a 1-line description, e.g. "Foundation in data, systems and programming — now applied to costing models, Excel automation and merchandising dashboards."

### Skills
- **Common weak points:** generic skills like "Microsoft Office", "Teamwork", "Leadership" pinned in the top spots.
- **Add:** the 30 skills in [02-profile-rewrite.md](02-profile-rewrite.md#d-skills), with the top 5 pinned. Remove anything that isn't related.

### Recommendations
- **Target:** at least 3 within 60 days. A recommendation from someone on the buyer side (a Nordic or US brand contact) is worth more than five from colleagues, because it shows that the client trusts you.
- **How to get them:** write a short draft for them (template in [05-networking-business-dev.md](05-networking-business-dev.md)); people say yes far more often when the writing is mostly done.

### Certifications
- Most merchandisers have none listed. Even 2–3 short, relevant ones help with search filters and credibility. Suggestions: sustainability/traceability (Textile Exchange courses, Higg FEM basics), Lean/Six Sigma Yellow Belt, Power BI (Microsoft PL-300 later on), Excel for business analysis.

### Featured
- **Usually empty.** Fill it with 3–5 items (list in [02-profile-rewrite.md](02-profile-rewrite.md#e-featured-section)).

### Activity
- **Common weak points:** only reposts, congratulations comments and job-change likes. To LinkedIn's algorithm and to recruiters, that looks like an inactive profile.
- **Fix:** 3 original posts a week plus 5 thoughtful comments a day (plan in [04-content-strategy.md](04-content-strategy.md)).

### Contact info and URL
- `linkedin.com/in/mahmudshowrov` is a clean custom URL. Keep it.
- **Add:** a professional email (not one that includes a nickname), a WhatsApp business number if you're comfortable sharing it, and your website, once it has a professional page.
- **Show_Tricks links:** only add them if the channel is about garments/merchandising. Otherwise they dilute the positioning.
- **Location:** "Dhaka, Bangladesh". Under "Open to" → **Finding a new job**, set the visibility to *Recruiters only* if you don't want your employer to see it. Add target titles and "Remote / On-site: Bangladesh + [target countries]".

## 1.3 Positioning gap analysis

How a typical **Senior Merchandiser profile in Bangladesh** compares with profiles that attract international recruiters and sourcing-office offers:

| Dimension | Typical BD Senior Merchandiser | High-performing international sourcing professional | Your gap (based on what you've told me) |
|-----------|-------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Identity | "Merchandiser" (an execution role) | "Sourcing / product development partner" (a commercial role) | Your skills are commercial (costing, negotiation), but titles alone read as execution. Lead with the commercial side. |
| Scope | One factory, one buyer | Multi-category, multi-vendor, market-specific | **You're strong here:** 4 categories, US and Nordic buyers. Say so clearly. |
| Proof | Duties | Numbers: FOB saved, OTD %, styles per season, order value | Likely missing. The biggest single gap. |
| Market knowledge | Silent | Talks about compliance, EU regulations (CSRD, Digital Product Passport, EUDR), US tariffs, nearshoring, cost trends | Nordic buyers care heavily about sustainability and traceability. Show you know it. |
| Visibility | Invisible: no posts | Regular, practical posts | Easy to fix. Your costing depth is excellent content. |
| Differentiator | None | A clear "only-I" angle | **Merchandiser + CSE + data/automation.** Few people have this. Use it. |
| Social proof | 0–1 recommendations | Buyer-side recommendations | Likely missing. |

### Comparison by target role

- **Merchandising Manager:** recruiters look for team leadership, P&L/margin ownership, and multi-buyer portfolio size. *What to show:* team coordination, how many juniors you guided, and the order book value you handled.
- **Apparel Sourcing Manager (brand or sourcing office):** vendor base development, cost negotiation, capacity planning, compliance, country-of-origin strategy. *What to show:* how many factories you've assessed or onboarded, and your negotiation results.
- **Product Development Manager:** proto-to-bulk lead time, fabric/trim development, tech pack interpretation, fit approvals. *What to show:* style counts and approval hit rates.
- **Global sourcing (buying office of an EU/US brand in BD):** this is likely **your best near-term fit.** Nordic Sourcing experience plus US buyers plus costing depth is exactly what Nordic and EU brand liaison offices in Dhaka hire for (Senior Merchandiser → Sourcing Manager / Product Manager roles).

## 1.4 Top 10 improvement priorities

In order of impact against effort:

1. **Rewrite the headline** (10 min). This is what most recruiters see first.
2. **Rewrite the About section** (30 min). Lead with the commercial value plus your CSE/data edge.
3. **Fill in real numbers** for the experience section (1–2 hours of digging through old emails and costing sheets). This is the biggest credibility jump.
4. **Restructure Skills:** add the 30, pin the top 5, and tag skills on each role.
5. **Banner** (1 hour in Canva).
6. **Photo** (if it's not already professional).
7. **Ask for 3 recommendations,** including one from the buyer side.
8. **Start the 3-posts-a-week rhythm,** beginning with the costing series, which is your strongest content.
9. **Build Featured:** a capability one-pager plus your 2 best posts plus a sample costing breakdown with confidential data removed.
10. **Set up "Open to":** recruiter-only visibility, target titles and target locations.
