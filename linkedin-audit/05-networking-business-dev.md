**Total: about 40 targeted requests a week.** Stay well under LinkedIn's weekly invitation limit (around 100–200, depending on your account). Quality matters more than volume: a high acceptance rate keeps your account healthy, so send to people with a clear reason to connect.

**Note:** free accounts can only send a few personalised connection notes each month. Use the notes for high-value targets (brand buyers, sourcing directors). For peers, connect without a note and send a message after they accept. LinkedIn Premium Career is worth it for 2–3 months while job-hunting; it gives unlimited notes, InMail and "Who viewed your profile".

## 5.2 Connection-message templates

**A. Brand buyer / sourcing director (EU, Nordic, US)**
> Hi [Name], I'm a Senior Merchandiser in Dhaka working with Nordic and US brands across knit, woven, denim and sweaters. I follow [Brand]'s sourcing work and would be glad to connect.

**B. Sourcing office manager in Dhaka**
> Hi [Name], I'm a Senior Merchandiser with 10+ years across knit, woven, denim and sweater, and I'd like to connect with sourcing professionals at [Company]. Happy to share notes on costing.

**C. Recruiter**

**D. Buying house owner**
> Hi [Name], I'm a Senior Merchandiser in Dhaka and I respect what you've built at [Company]. I'd love to connect and learn from how you've grown it.

**E. Factory owner/director**
> Hi [Name], I'm a Senior Merchandiser working with EU and US brands across knit, woven, denim and sweaters, and I'm keen to connect with strong manufacturers like [Factory].

**F. After engaging with their post (the warmest approach)**
> Hi [Name], I liked your point on [specific topic] in your post on [X]. It matches what I see on the merchandising side. Would be glad to connect.

## 5.3 Follow-up sequence (after they accept)

| When | Message | Goal |
|------|---------|------|
| Day 0 (on acceptance) | "Thanks for connecting, [Name]. I post practical notes on garment costing and sourcing. Happy to swap ideas anytime." | Friendly; no pitch |
| Day 7–14 | Comment meaningfully on 1–2 of their posts | Get noticed |
| Day 21–30 | Send something useful: "Thought this might be useful for your team — [carousel on X]." | Give value first |
| Day 45+ | Only with a clear reason: "[Company] is growing its [category] sourcing. I'd value 15 minutes to learn how your team works with suppliers in Bangladesh." | A real conversation |

**Recruiter follow-up (when there's a role):**
> Hi [Name], I saw the [Role] opening at [Company]. My background in [category] costing and vendor management for [market] brands fits well. My profile has the details, and I can share a CV. Would a short call this week work?

**Recommendation request (send it with a draft):**
> Hi [Name], I'm updating my LinkedIn profile and would really value a short recommendation from you, since we worked together on [project/buyer]. To make it easy, here's a draft you can edit freely: "[2–3 sentences on a specific thing you did together]". Thank you. Happy to return the favour.

## 5.4 How LinkedIn builds toward NextGen Garments Ltd.

A buying house sells **trust**: brands are trusting you with their money, timeline and reputation in a country they can't watch every day. LinkedIn is the cheapest way to build that trust *before* you launch.

**Phase 1: Now to month 12 (as an employee)**
- Build a personal reputation for costing expertise. Post consistently.
- Grow a network of brand-side buyers in your target markets (Nordic/EU small and mid-size brands are realistic first clients, because big brands rarely use new buying houses).
- Map factories you trust, by category, capacity, compliance status and MOQ. Keep this private.
- **Be careful:** while employed at Nordic Sourcing, don't contact your employer's clients about your future business, and don't announce plans publicly. Check your employment contract for non-compete and non-solicit clauses. Breaking them would hurt your reputation more than any post could help it.

**Phase 2: Pre-launch (3–6 months before)**
- Create the NextGen Garments Ltd. company page, but keep it quiet until you're ready.
- Prepare a capability deck covering categories, factory base, compliance approach, costing transparency, QA process and logistics.
- Choose a niche. "We do everything" loses to "Nordic-standard knit and sweater sourcing for small and mid-size EU brands, MOQ from 500 per style." A niche makes your LinkedIn targeting much easier.

**Phase 3: Launch and growth**
- Update your headline to option #5.
- Post case studies (with client permission): problem → approach → outcome.
- Put the team on LinkedIn too; merchandisers posting about the company multiplies reach.
- Use the content you've built as proof: "Here's how we cost. Here's how we handle delays."

**Challenge to your thinking:** many buying houses in Bangladesh compete on commission alone, and the race to 3–4% commission is brutal. Your edge could be **cost transparency plus data**: open-book costing, live order dashboards for clients, and should-cost analysis. Few buying houses offer that, and the brands that care about sustainability and traceability value it. Your CSE background makes it credible. Build the content strategy around this, and the business positioning will follow.

## 5.5 Competitor benchmarking

**Why I haven't named 5 profiles:** I can't access LinkedIn from here, and I won't make up profiles, headlines or engagement figures. Here is a 30-minute method so you can do it properly yourself. Paste what you find and I'll analyse it.

**How to find the 5:**
1. Search LinkedIn **Posts** for "garment costing", "apparel sourcing Bangladesh", "merchandising tips" and "denim sourcing". Sort by most relevant and filter for the past month.
2. Pick people who post regularly **and** get comments from industry people (not just likes). Aim for a mix:
   - 2 Bangladesh-based merchandising/sourcing professionals with an active audience
   - 1 sourcing director/head of sourcing at an EU or US brand
   - 1 buying house founder who posts
   - 1 global apparel supply chain voice (consultant, analyst or educator)
3. Write down the details in this table:

| Name / URL | Headline (copy exactly) | Positioning in 5 words | Posts per week | Typical format | Main topics | Keywords they use | Avg. comments per post | What I can learn | What I'll do differently |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------------|---------------:|----------------|-------------|-------------------|----------------------:|------------------|--------------------------|
| | | | | | | | | | |

**What to look for:**
- **Headline:** do they lead with title, value or audience?
- **Content:** is it teaching (frameworks, numbers) or just opinions and news?
- **Audience:** who comments? Buyers, peers or students?
- **Gaps:** almost nobody posts *real costing logic* with formulas. That's your opening.
